# Supplementary clustering experiment

Three unsupervised algorithms were evaluated on impact-profile features: K-Means, DBSCAN and Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM). The target class (Low/Moderate/High) and target-defining labels were not used. Numeric impact fields were coerced to numeric, negative values treated as missing, transformed with log1p, median-imputed and standardised. The `estimatedLoss_reported` missingness indicator was excluded. A reproducible 5,000-record fit sample and 2,500-record evaluation subset were selected with seed 42. Hyperparameter candidates were evaluated using silhouette (higher is better), Davies-Bouldin (lower is better), and Calinski-Harabasz (higher is better); DBSCAN noise points were excluded from internal cluster-quality scores and their fraction reported separately.

Best tested configurations by silhouette: K-Means k=2 (0.9068; DB=0.8742; CH=594.77); DBSCAN eps=0.8, min_samples=10 (0.5839; DB=0.5550; CH=553.74; noise=6.32%); GMM components=2 (0.5448; DB=2.0167; CH=293.15). These scores are internal geometric measures, not predictive accuracy or proof that clusters correspond to official disaster severity. The two-cluster K-Means result should be interpreted as a broad separation in recorded-impact profiles, not as the paper's three-level target. Results are supplementary and exploratory.
